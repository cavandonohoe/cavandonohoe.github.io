# Methods and preregistration · version 1

## Scope and estimands

Compare the first English-language novel in each series: Martin (1996), Maas (2015).
The user explicitly corrected an initial HBO-versus-novel specification to book one versus
book one. No television evidence enters these results. Runtime, episode, and season
metrics are not applicable. No cross-media calibration is necessary for this corrected scope.

The main estimands are unique sexual-content encounters/references per 10,000 words;
sexual-content word-span share; category-specific counts; and the proposed scene index.
Word counts must come from the same selected edition's narrative text, excluding front
matter, appendices, advertisements and author notes. The GOT prologue is narrative text
and stays in the denominator. Chapters vary greatly in length: chapter order percent is
not percentage of narrative words. Page counts cannot substitute for word counts.

## Annotation unit

A scene is a continuous narrative situation, split at a substantial time, location, or
viewpoint transition. Do not split solely because sexual content begins or ends. Agree on
the scene inventory before scoring. Chapters are reporting units, not interchangeable scenes.
Within a scene, one contiguous encounter or reference is an event; one encounter can have
multiple categories. Several reported acts within an encounter do not imply several
distinct countable events. A guide summarizing a collective gathering becomes an unresolved
cluster until primary review identifies the actual boundaries.

Unique event ID + all source references prevents the same occurrence being counted once
per source. Duplicate IDs with conflicting annotations fail validation. Category totals are
multi-label and are not summed to claim a total number of distinct events. Overlapping
word spans use the union of half-open intervals within each scene.

## Categories and explicitness

`configs/taxonomy.json` specifies all nine requested categories and subtypes, distinguishing
casual affection, nonsexual nudity, implied versus directly narrated acts, and coercion.
Severity is ordinal 0–5, with null for unassessed. Zero is never missing. Anchor levels
describe explicitness and descriptive detail; they do not rank the moral seriousness of
an assault. Severity needs book-specific examples established from lawful texts and
independent adjudication before it can be called validated.

The pilot stores a provisional `explicit_activity` label with subtype
`activity_reported_explicitness_unresolved` when a guide reports activity but the original
depiction is unavailable. This is a candidate for adjudication, **not evidence of explicit
prose**. All severities are null. The dashboard explicitly calls these labels provisional.

Consent is consensual, coercive, violent, ambiguous, unknown, or not applicable. A thought
or explanatory conversation can be not applicable; a person's desire does not establish
consent to subsequent contact. Marriage and absence of resistance do not establish consent.
Retain age context. Keep classifications non-graphic and separate coercive material from
romantic content. No clinical or legal judgments are implied by this research taxonomy.

## Evidence hierarchy

Primary-verified = the original novel passage was assessed and severity assigned.
Source-verified = a specific secondary source supports an event lead; original detail remains
unverified. Candidate = automatic or otherwise unresolved lead. No current event is
primary-verified. `detection=llm` identifies the interactive assistant's guide review;
there has been no independent human verification. The exact interactive model build is not
available, so annotations are reproduced from committed snapshots, not regenerated by an LLM.

All pilot confidence scores are null: they are not calibrated probabilities. A future
numeric confidence threshold cannot replace source review. Re-run lexical candidate
detection with the frozen rule config, exact private text hashes, and versioned inputs.
`minimum_pattern_hits` controls lexical candidate thresholding. No programmatic LLM provider
is enabled; any future provider must record model/version, prompt hash and full private
response cache. Do not claim deterministic regeneration from a provider's temperature alone.

## Rates and proposed index

On fully reviewed scenes, unique event count / matched narrative words × 10,000 gives
events per 10,000 words. Severity-weighted rate replaces each event with its maximum
category severity, so labels don't multiply its contribution. Scene prevalence counts each
scene once if it contains any eligible sexual-content event. Sexual word-span share is
the union of annotated sexual-content spans / matched words. Missing spans yield null.

The five scene components use maximum severity / 5: acts (implied + explicit), anatomy
(sexualized nudity/anatomical description), intimacy (romantic/sexual kissing + touching),
language (dialogue + thoughts), and intensity (maximum across sexual labels). Use the
starting weights 35/25/20/10/10. Scene maxima avoid additive inflation from overlapping
labels, but lose encounter frequency; raw counts and word rates must accompany the index.
Intensity deliberately reuses severity and is correlated with other components: these are
not five independent measurements. Test the alternatives in `weights.json`.

Score each work separately. The all-content channel retains violence/coercion; the consensual
channel includes only explicitly consensual events; the coercive channel includes coercive
and violent events. Not-applicable dialogue/thoughts are retained in the all-content channel,
not automatically counted as consensual encounters. Descriptive category tables retain
nonsexual affection and nudity, which contribute zero to the sexual index.

No fixed scale has been psychometrically calibrated. Even a completed score is conditional
on the taxonomy, scene boundaries, severity judgments, scope and weights. Do not infer
pleasure, erotic intent or moral harm from the number.

## Uncertainty and missing data

Without a complete scene inventory, no exposure-based whole-book index is identifiable:
return null. With a complete inventory of N scenes and reviewed scene score sum S,
logical bounds are [S/N, (S+100×unreviewed scenes)/N]. These are worst-case missingness
bounds, **not confidence intervals** and not evidence that missingness is random.

The purposive secondary-source pilot has unknown selection probabilities and unmeasured
omission error. Do not bootstrap it, attach a binomial interval to it, extrapolate its
counts, or use confidence-weighted pseudo-counts. Full-book annotation is a census of these
two works, not a random sample of novels. Future uncertainty should target annotation error
or a clearly defined resampling target, not pretend that the two books were randomly sampled.

For validation with an actual random held-out sample, estimate category precision/recall
against independently adjudicated scene-level labels. Wilson intervals are available for
appropriate binomial denominators, with clustering/design effects handled separately.
Do not apply a simple binomial interval to unequal-probability stratified estimates.

## Validation protocol to execute after text access

Freeze a private train/pilot versus held-out split by scene before tuning. Stratify each
novel by early/middle/late narrative position and category, and include scenes with no
lexical hits. A candidate-only audit cannot estimate recall. The queue sampler supports
strata and negatives; retain selection probabilities and population stratum sizes.

Use at least two independent annotators for agreement; one model reviewing its own labels
is not independent replication. Adjudicate disagreements only after initial labels are
frozen. Report scene-level binary confusion matrices per category; event-level matching
requires a prespecified boundary-overlap criterion, not the binary helper alone. Compute
Cohen's kappa only for independently labeled matched units, and weighted ordinal agreement
for severity if appropriate. Current precision, recall, agreement and calibration are all
unavailable. Tests use explicitly synthetic fixtures and do not estimate novel accuracy.

Candidate detector counterexamples to include: a kiss on a relative's cheek, metaphorical
desire, bare feet, a nonsexual embrace, and references to an assault versus an enacted
assault. Ambiguous cues must be reviewed rather than treated as keyword-count events.
