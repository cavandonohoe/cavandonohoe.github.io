Which TV Shows Have Exceptional Episodes?

Some shows have an episode that everyone seems to agree is extraordinary. How rare is that? I looked beyond the IMDb Top 250 at all TV series and miniseries with at least 25,000 series votes in IMDb’s official downloadable datasets. An exceptional episode earns at least 9.7/10 with at least 5,000 episode votes.

Which shows have the most?

This is an episode count, so longer shows have more chances to qualify. It is not a ranking of overall show quality. The labels show each count and its series-vote total; the full searchable table follows. On phones, labels sit above thicker bars that use the available chart width.

Explore the shows

All 1,353 eligible shows are included, even those with zero exceptional episodes. Search for a title or genre. On narrow screens, expand a show’s row to see all details and vote cutoffs. “Saved Top 250” refers to historical membership, not today’s chart.

Exceptional share = 100 × exceptional episodes ÷ total episodes. The table’s percentage uses the primary cutoff: rating ≥9.7 with ≥5,000 episode votes. “Total episodes” means every episode linked to the show in IMDb’s snapshot, including specials, unrated entries, and any upcoming placeholders. It is not restricted to episodes that received enough votes. “Rated episodes” counts the linked episodes that have any IMDb rating record, as a supplemental reference for shows with many unrated specials or placeholders.

Which shows have the highest exceptional share?

This view divides the primary exceptional-episode count by the show’s total episode count. Short shows can rank highly with only a few standout episodes. The labels show the numerator, denominator, percentage, and how many of the show’s episodes have a rating. Shows without an exceptional episode are omitted from this chart and remain in the full table above.

How sensitive is the result to episode votes?

The rating cutoff stays at 9.7 and the series-vote cutoff stays at 25,000. Only the minimum number of episode votes changes.

Comparison with the saved Top 250

The existing CSV contains 250 distinct show IDs. Of those, 218 meet the 25,000-series-vote cutoff in this snapshot. This comparison uses official episode ratings for those historical IDs. I haven’t rebuilt the chart by sorting bulk ratings: IMDb’s Top 250 uses its own ranking rules.

The cohort rows overlap: eligible saved members and eligible shows outside the saved list together make up the broader universe. The all-member saved cohort also includes 32 shows below the series-vote cutoff.

Sources, validation, and limitations

The universe comes from IMDb’s official non-commercial datasets: title.basics identifies series and miniseries, title.episode links episodes to their parent shows, and title.ratings provides weighted ratings and vote counts. Series votes are for the show itself; they are not a sum of episode votes. There are no language, country, animation, or documentary exclusions.

Existing CSVs serve as validation checks. The stored Top 250 CSV has 15,519 rows; 15,408 match a unique official series/season/episode key. 22 matched rows change their primary exceptional classification between snapshots. Because the stored CSV lacks episode IDs, matching is provisional and can be affected by renumbering as well as changes in ratings and votes.

Specials and season-zero entries are included. The downloads lack exact episode air dates, so this analysis does not apply an exact release-date filter. A rating record is required. Vote cutoffs reduce the influence of very small samples but do not eliminate fandom effects or organized voting.
