The comparison is **A Game of Thrones (1996) versus A Court of Thorns and Roses (2015)**,
first novels only. No television observations or later-series material are included.

Chapter counts are documented by the public guide tables of contents. The reviewed guide
pages are GOT chapters 3, 6, 8, 11, 23 and ACOTAR chapters 2, 21, 27, 42, 46. The review
was purposive: it is not a representative or complete sample. Guides can omit encounters,
nudity, affection and thoughts. Two reviewed summaries produced no retained event leads;
that is not a finding of no sexual content in their original chapters.

The guide for GOT chapter 8 supports a lead for an adult encounter witnessed by Bran.
Chapter 11 supports a collective wedding-feast cluster and a separate child-marriage
encounter; the exact number of feast encounters is unresolved. Chapter 23 supplies a
conversation lead. Original explicitness is unassessed.

For ACOTAR, chapter 21 supplies conversation, desire and contact leads; chapter 27 supplies
one encounter cluster despite a guide mentioning repeated activity. Chapter 42 contains
separate reunion and forced-kiss leads. Chapter 46 supplies a final activity lead.
Each is linked to its source in `data/events.jsonl`; no scene prose is redistributed.

Seven versus four is **not a result about which novel contains more sexual content**.
These counts reflect what was retained from different, incomplete, deliberately selected
secondary summaries. No uncertainty interval can repair that selection and measurement bias.

The most informative current charts are the chapter maps and denominator audit. They show
where leads exist and how much measurement remains. The category and consent charts describe
the annotation pilot alone. The heatmap measures guide-review coverage, never inferred
sexual intensity. Empty severity and sensitivity panels explain what evidence is missing.

Verdict: **the jury requests two lawful novels and a functioning denominator.**
There is no supported winner or quantitative difference yet.
